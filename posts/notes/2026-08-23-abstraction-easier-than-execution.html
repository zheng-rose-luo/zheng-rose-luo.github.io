Abstraction Is Always Easier Than Execution

Saying it abstractly is always far easier than actually making it happen.

This thought came to me while watching a short video in which someone said, "Here's a puzzle," and then proceeded to advance the plot by revealing the solution — without ever stating what the puzzle actually was.

It reminds me of the saying from the last era: talk is cheap, show me the code. Saying "this is the greatest work in the world" is always easier than actually creating the greatest work in the world. It also brings to mind Lu Xun's critique of using phrases like "as beautiful as peach blossoms" to describe a wound — that kind of rhetoric that glosses over harsh realities.

And that is why I strive to be a doer.

抽象地说出来总是比真的做出来要简单很多

抽象地说出来总是比真的做出来要简单很多。

看到某个短视频里说“啊这里有一个谜题”，然后沿着谜底推进剧情——但实际上根本没写谜题内容，有感。

令我想起上个时代说的 talk is cheap, show me the code。说“这是世界上最伟大的作品”，总是比做出“世界上最伟大的作品”要难。也令我想起鲁迅先生批判的用“艳若桃花”形容伤口，那种粉饰太平的文辞。

所以我致力于做实干家。
