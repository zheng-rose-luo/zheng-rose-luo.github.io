# Understanding brains, building adaptive intelligence

My long-term ambition connects two goals: understanding the computational principles of the brain, and using those insights to develop more capable artificial intelligence. Learning, flexible behaviour and neural dynamics are starting points for this work.

## How does experience change computation?

What changes within a network as it learns, and which changes enable it to use previous experience in unfamiliar situations? I am interested in relating learning dynamics and internal representations to observable behaviour.

A useful explanation should distinguish between competing accounts. For example, two systems can perform similarly on familiar examples while transferring very differently to a new context. Studying that difference may reveal what each system has actually learned.

## How do neural dynamics support flexible behaviour?

Neural activity evolves over time, even when the external input is unchanged. I am curious about how population dynamics maintain information and internal states, and how these states shape subsequent behaviour.

I would like to connect manageable dynamical models with measurements and interventions. The aim is to understand which features of a model are needed to explain a phenomenon, and where the model fails.

## Which principles can improve artificial learning?

Biological systems motivate questions about learning from limited experience, retaining knowledge, adapting to changing conditions and operating under resource constraints. I would like to investigate whether a specific computational principle can yield a measurable improvement in an artificial system.

These are candidate directions rather than a promise to solve all of them at once. For an individual project, I would choose a defined capability and comparison: for example, transfer under a controlled change in the task, or adaptation under a matched training budget. My earlier work on neuronal information and energy measures also sustains an interest in the costs of neural computation.

## Moving between models and experiments

I am drawn both to finding regularities in complex networks and to studying simpler systems with controllable conditions. Small models can make assumptions explicit; larger models can reveal additional phenomena and help test the reach of an explanation. Behavioural and neural observations should help shape the question from the beginning.

Artificial and biological systems need not implement the same mechanism. Similarities can suggest hypotheses, while differences can expose the roles of architecture, learning history and biological constraints. I would evaluate a model’s explanatory value for neuroscience separately from its usefulness for improving AI.

## The experimental training I want

I want to participate in experiments as well as analyse their results: helping design and pilot tasks, collect data, assess its quality, and understand the limitations of the measurement. I am interested in research environments where theoretical predictions and experimental decisions are developed together.

My work in mechanics has taught me how strongly the interpretation of a result depends on specimen preparation, measurement choices and model assumptions. I hope to build on that experience through systematic training in neuroscience and machine learning.
